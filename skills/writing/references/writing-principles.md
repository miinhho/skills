# Applied writing principles

Select principles by the reader's task. Clarity, completeness, and voice can require different choices in different genres.

## Purpose and audience

A useful central point guides selection without forcing every genre into a thesis statement. An explanation builds understanding; a report supports judgment; instructions support action. A personal essay can explore an experience without ending in advice.

Distinguish the author's knowledge from the reader's. Supply a definition, situation, example, or missing relationship at the point it becomes necessary. Do not explain every term merely because it is technical: an expert may need the changed condition rather than an introductory lesson.

## Claims and development

A paragraph needs enough development for its purpose. A claim may require evidence and an explanation of the inference; a procedure may require a precondition and an observable result. Neither is improved by compression that leaves the reader reconstructing omitted steps.

For example, “Reservations fell after the fee changed, so the fee caused the decline” needs more than a citation to reservation counts. The counts establish a sequence, not the exclusion of competing causes. State the observed association unless the sources support causation.

Examples clarify a concept but do not automatically establish its frequency or generality. Label hypothetical scenarios when they might be mistaken for real evidence. Keep personal observations in the author's own evidential register rather than converting them to quantified research claims.

Counterarguments belong where they affect the conclusion. Do not append generic “there are challenges” paragraphs or limitations unrelated to what was claimed.

## Structure and cohesion

Choose an order the reader can follow:

- A recommendation can lead with the decision, then grounds and tradeoffs.
- An unfamiliar concept can begin with a concrete problem, introduce the concept, then explain how it addresses that problem.
- Instructions can follow the action sequence, with prerequisites before dependent steps.
- A reflective piece can follow an experience without adopting report headings.

These are options, not mandatory templates. A paragraph's first sentence often orients the reader, but a deliberately built example can lead to its point later. A summary helps readers navigate a long document; repeating the same conclusion after each short section does not.

Explain the actual relationship between paragraphs: cause, contrast, qualification, example, or next step. Avoid merely adding connective words. Keep important exceptions near the affected instruction, not in a distant caveat footer.

## Precision, economy, and voice

Concrete language names what happened or what the reader should do. It does not require a number in every sentence. When the actor is unknown, a passive construction can be more honest than inventing one. Negative phrasing is necessary when prohibitions or exclusions are the point.

Cut duplicated claims and empty modifiers before shortening substantive explanations. A longer sentence can express a condition more clearly than two fragments whose relationship is unclear. Keep terminology consistent instead of rotating synonyms for stylistic variety.

Preserve the author's register and attitude. Do not turn a skeptical observation into a sales pitch, an uncertain experience into a categorical conclusion, or a personal account into institutional prose. Style samples guide wording, not permission to invent matching experiences.
