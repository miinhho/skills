# Review an issue as a contributor

Use these questions to assess the actual request and available sources. They are not a required issue template or a count-based scoring system.

## Is the work understandable without the conversation?

The title and opening identify the affected behavior or requested capability. The issue explains the problem, relevant conditions, and why it matters without relying on chat history, unexplained references, or a log of the author's activity. Links support the description instead of replacing it.

## Does the content match the current scope?

Distinguish current agreements, superseded requests, and open proposals. Preserve the requested work type: bugs, features, improvements, and testing tasks have different purposes. Do not omit requested coverage because the behavior already exists or silently add product work to a testing task.

## Are consequential details preserved?

Keep relevant conditions, impact, expected outcomes, diagnostic artifacts, technical rationale, scope boundaries, and compatibility or accessibility requirements. Compare revisions with their originals. Simplifying prose or removing one paragraph does not authorize deleting useful neighboring material.

## Do claims match the evidence?

Check versions, steps, frequencies, commands, outputs, causes, and source references against the supplied or observed information. Do not claim someone else's observation as an independent reproduction. Keep proposals and hypotheses distinguishable from facts without imposing a completed-diagnosis requirement.

Retain limitations that change what evidence establishes. Omit unrelated caveats and routine status notices. If essential facts conflict or are missing, ask a focused question outside the draft rather than inventing an answer.

## Does the structure serve the reader and project?

Use the current project template where available. Choose optional sections to fit the issue; do not force all types into a bug report. Avoid filler, repeated lists, arbitrary length limits, and manual hard wrapping. Answer testing questions with future actions and expected observations rather than historical results alone.

## Does the proposed work respect constraints?

Connect proposals to the stated problem. Preserve required behavior and explain a meaningful tradeoff or open decision where relevant. Do not present a workaround as the agreed final solution, discard compatibility guarantees, or broaden the issue into unrelated cleanup.

## Is source material handled appropriately?

Exclude secrets and unnecessary identifying or confidential details from public-facing drafts. Do not turn a private discussion or user-specific account into reusable examples or evaluations without explicit permission. Synthetic examples should be independently constructed, not recognizable retellings with different names.

## Is the response the requested artifact?

Return title and body for a draft, concrete findings for a review, or the requested focused edit. Respect output language and format. Keep author-facing questions outside the body. Do not modify unrelated files, publish remotely, or claim publication merely because content was requested.
